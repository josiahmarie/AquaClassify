PLASTIC POLLUTION IN AQUATIC ENVIRONMENTS HAS BECOME ONE OF THE MOST PRESSING ENVIRONMENTAL ISSUES WORLDWIDE. THE EXCESSIVE USE OF PLASTIC PRODUCTS, COUPLED WITH IMPROPER WASTE DISPOSAL, HAS LED TO THE ACCUMULATION OF PLASTIC DEBRIS IN RIVERS, LAKES, AND OCEANS, SEVERELY IMPACTING MARINE LIFE, WATER QUALITY, AND ECOSYSTEM BALANCE. AS AN ARCHIPELAGIC NATION WITH VAST COASTAL AND FRESHWATER RESOURCES, THE PHILIPPINES SUFFERS GREATLY FROM PLASTIC WASTE ACCUMULATION, THREATENING BIODIVERSITY AND LOCAL COMMUNITIES THAT RELY ON THESE WATER BODIES FOR SUSTENANCE AND LIVELIHOOD. THE PERSISTENCE OF PLASTIC WASTE IN AQUATIC ENVIRONMENTS IS PARTICULARLY ALARMING DUE TO ITS SLOW DEGRADATION RATE, ALLOWING MICROPLASTICS TO INFILTRATE FOOD CHAINS AND CONTAMINATE WATER SUPPLIES.
